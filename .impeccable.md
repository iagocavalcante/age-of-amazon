## Design Context

### Users
Age of Amazon serves a global audience spanning experienced classic-RTS players and casual newcomers. Players should be able to read units, resources, threats, and commands immediately, while experienced players retain the speed, density, and control expected from a traditional real-time strategy game.

### Brand Personality
Dense, dangerous, and mysterious. The rainforest should feel alive and strategically consequential: beautiful enough to invite exploration, threatening enough to command respect, and never reducible to a decorative grassland backdrop.

### Aesthetic Direction
Rich painted pixel art for a pan-Indigenous Americas historical fantasy anchored in the Amazon rainforest. The world may draw from Amazonian peoples as well as Maya, Aztec, and other major Indigenous American civilizations, including monumental Mesoamerican architecture. Each influence should remain identifiable and intentionally attributed instead of being collapsed into a generic “tribal” mixture. Ecology should remain recognizably Amazonian where the game is set, while faction architecture, tools, clothing, and monuments may express distinct cultural or fictionalized regional identities. Protected or sacred designs should not be copied. The game may use richer painted assets even when they increase the build beyond the earlier procedural-art baseline, provided performance and cross-platform play remain practical.

### Design Principles
1. Make the jungle the dominant visual character through density, vertical layers, occlusion, atmosphere, wildlife, water, and environmental motion.
2. Preserve classic-RTS readability: silhouettes, team colors, selection states, threats, and interactive resources must remain legible at gameplay zoom.
3. Welcome newcomers through visible affordances, contextual feedback, and progressive disclosure without slowing expert play.
4. Keep the ecology unmistakably Amazonian while giving Amazonian, Maya, Aztec, and other inspirations distinct visual identities; never present them as one interchangeable generic culture.
5. Spend visual richness on strategically meaningful elements first—biome boundaries, hazards, resources, units, buildings, combat, and fog-state transitions.
